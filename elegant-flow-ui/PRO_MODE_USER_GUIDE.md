# Pro Mode User Guide

## Overview

Pro Mode is an advanced interface that gives you complete, granular control over every aspect of your product image generation. Unlike Standard Mode, which uses AI to interpret your simple descriptions, Pro Mode provides a "Direct Line" to the image generation engine, allowing you to precisely define every detail of your scene.

## When to Use Pro Mode

Use Pro Mode when you need:

- **Precise Control**: Exact specifications for lighting, camera angles, and composition
- **Complex Scenes**: Multiple objects with specific relationships and positioning
- **Professional Results**: Consistent, repeatable outputs for product catalogs
- **Advanced Customization**: Fine-tuned aesthetic and photographic characteristics

## Interface Overview

Pro Mode organizes controls into five collapsible sections:

1. **Scene & Style** - Overall scene description and artistic direction
2. **Lighting** - Light conditions, direction, and shadow characteristics
3. **Aesthetics** - Composition, color scheme, and mood
4. **Camera** - Photographic characteristics and technical settings
5. **Object Builder** - Define multiple objects with detailed properties

## Section Guide

### 1. Scene & Style

This section defines the overall context and artistic direction of your image.

#### Short Description

A concise overview of what you want to create.

**Example:**

```
A premium smartphone displayed on a minimalist surface with elegant lighting
```

**Tips:**

- Keep it focused on the main subject
- Mention the primary product or focal point
- Include the general setting or environment

#### Background Setting

Detailed description of the environment and surroundings.

**Example:**

```
A clean, modern studio environment with a white marble surface. 
Soft gradient background transitioning from light gray to white. 
Minimal props to maintain focus on the product.
```

**Tips:**

- Describe surfaces, materials, and textures
- Specify background colors and gradients
- Mention any environmental elements (studio, outdoor, interior)
- Keep the focus on supporting the product, not overwhelming it

#### Style Medium

The visual style or medium you want to emulate.

**Examples:**

- `Professional product photography`
- `Editorial magazine style`
- `Lifestyle photography`
- `Minimalist commercial photography`
- `High-end catalog photography`

#### Artistic Style

Specific artistic influences or aesthetic approaches.

**Examples:**

- `Clean and modern`
- `Luxury and sophisticated`
- `Warm and inviting`
- `Bold and dramatic`
- `Soft and ethereal`

#### Context

Additional contextual information about the scene or usage.

**Example:**

```
Product hero shot for e-commerce listing, emphasizing premium quality 
and modern design aesthetic
```

**Tips:**

- Mention the intended use (e-commerce, advertising, catalog)
- Specify the target audience or market segment
- Include any brand positioning information

### 2. Lighting

Control how your product is illuminated.

#### Conditions

The type and quality of lighting.

**Examples:**

- `Soft studio lighting with diffused sources`
- `Natural daylight from large windows`
- `Dramatic side lighting with high contrast`
- `Even, shadowless lighting`
- `Golden hour warm lighting`

**Tips:**

- Specify soft vs. hard light
- Mention light sources (studio, natural, artificial)
- Describe the overall lighting quality

#### Direction

Where the light is coming from.

**Examples:**

- `Front lighting with slight overhead angle`
- `45-degree side lighting from camera left`
- `Backlit with rim lighting`
- `Three-point lighting setup`
- `Top-down overhead lighting`

**Tips:**

- Use clock positions or degrees for precision
- Mention multiple light sources if applicable
- Specify primary and fill light positions

#### Shadows

Shadow characteristics and behavior.

**Examples:**

- `Soft, diffused shadows with gradual falloff`
- `Minimal shadows, almost shadowless`
- `Crisp, defined shadows for depth`
- `Long shadows extending to the right`
- `Subtle contact shadows only`

**Tips:**

- Describe shadow softness (hard, soft, diffused)
- Specify shadow direction and length
- Mention shadow intensity (subtle, pronounced, dramatic)

### 3. Aesthetics

Define the artistic and compositional elements.

#### Composition

How elements are arranged in the frame.

**Examples:**

- `Centered composition with negative space`
- `Rule of thirds with product in left third`
- `Diagonal composition for dynamic energy`
- `Symmetrical, balanced layout`
- `Asymmetrical with visual tension`

**Tips:**

- Reference composition rules (rule of thirds, golden ratio)
- Specify balance and symmetry
- Mention negative space usage

#### Color Scheme

The color palette and color relationships.

**Examples:**

- `Monochromatic whites and grays`
- `Warm earth tones with gold accents`
- `Cool blues and silvers`
- `High contrast black and white`
- `Pastel palette with soft pinks and blues`

**Tips:**

- Specify dominant colors
- Mention accent colors
- Describe color temperature (warm, cool, neutral)
- Reference color harmonies (complementary, analogous)

#### Mood & Atmosphere

The emotional quality and feeling of the image.

**Examples:**

- `Clean, professional, and trustworthy`
- `Luxurious, exclusive, and aspirational`
- `Warm, inviting, and approachable`
- `Bold, energetic, and modern`
- `Calm, serene, and minimalist`

**Tips:**

- Use descriptive adjectives
- Think about the emotional response you want
- Consider your brand personality

### 4. Camera

Control the technical photographic characteristics.

#### Camera Angle

The viewpoint and perspective.

**Examples:**

- `Eye-level, straight-on view`
- `Slight overhead angle at 30 degrees`
- `Low angle looking up at product`
- `Three-quarter view from upper right`
- `Flat lay, directly overhead`

**Tips:**

- Specify vertical angle (overhead, eye-level, low)
- Mention horizontal position (front, side, three-quarter)
- Use degrees for precision

#### Lens & Focal Length

The lens characteristics and focal length.

**Examples:**

- `50mm standard lens for natural perspective`
- `85mm portrait lens for compression`
- `24mm wide angle for environmental context`
- `100mm macro lens for detail shots`
- `35mm for slight wide perspective`

**Tips:**

- Standard lenses (35-50mm) for natural look
- Longer lenses (85-135mm) for compression and isolation
- Macro lenses for extreme detail
- Wide lenses (24-35mm) for context and environment

#### Depth of Field

How much of the image is in focus.

**Examples:**

- `Shallow depth of field, f/2.8, blurred background`
- `Deep depth of field, f/11, everything sharp`
- `Medium depth of field, f/5.6, gradual falloff`
- `Very shallow DOF, f/1.4, extreme bokeh`
- `Infinite focus, f/16, front to back sharpness`

**Tips:**

- Lower f-numbers (f/1.4-f/2.8) = shallow DOF, blurred backgrounds
- Higher f-numbers (f/8-f/16) = deep DOF, more in focus
- Mention bokeh quality if relevant

#### Focus

What is sharp and what is not.

**Examples:**

- `Sharp focus on product front face`
- `Focus on product label and branding`
- `Selective focus on product details`
- `Everything in sharp focus`
- `Focus on foreground product, background soft`

**Tips:**

- Specify the exact focus point
- Mention what should be sharp vs. soft
- Describe focus falloff if applicable

### 5. Object Builder

Define multiple objects in your scene with precise control.

#### Adding Objects

Click **"Add New Object"** to create a new object card. Each object has seven properties:

#### Description

Detailed description of the object.

**Example:**

```
A premium smartphone with a sleek aluminum frame and edge-to-edge 
OLED display showing a vibrant gradient wallpaper
```

**Tips:**

- Be specific about materials and finishes
- Mention key visual features
- Describe the object's state or condition

#### Location

Where the object is positioned in the scene.

**Examples:**

- `Center of frame on marble surface`
- `Left third of composition, slightly forward`
- `Background, upper right corner`
- `Foreground, bottom center`

**Tips:**

- Use frame positions (center, left, right, top, bottom)
- Mention depth (foreground, midground, background)
- Be specific about placement

#### Relationship

How this object relates to other objects.

**Examples:**

- `Main subject, hero product`
- `Supporting prop next to main product`
- `Background element providing context`
- `Overlapping the main product slightly`
- `Positioned behind and to the left of primary object`

**Tips:**

- Specify if it's the main subject or supporting element
- Describe spatial relationships to other objects
- Mention any overlapping or layering

#### Relative Size

The size of this object compared to others.

**Examples:**

- `Large, dominant in frame`
- `Medium size, balanced with other elements`
- `Small accent piece`
- `Life-size representation`
- `Oversized for dramatic effect`

**Tips:**

- Use relative terms (large, medium, small)
- Compare to other objects if multiple exist
- Mention if size is realistic or stylized

#### Shape & Color

Visual characteristics of form and color.

**Examples:**

- `Rectangular with rounded corners, midnight blue finish`
- `Cylindrical bottle, transparent glass with amber liquid`
- `Spherical, matte white surface`
- `Organic curved form, rose gold metallic`

**Tips:**

- Describe basic shape (rectangular, cylindrical, spherical, organic)
- Specify colors and finishes
- Mention any distinctive shape features

#### Texture

Surface quality and tactile characteristics.

**Examples:**

- `Smooth, glossy glass surface`
- `Brushed aluminum with fine grain`
- `Matte, soft-touch coating`
- `Polished chrome, highly reflective`
- `Textured fabric with visible weave`

**Tips:**

- Describe surface finish (glossy, matte, satin)
- Mention material texture
- Specify reflectivity

#### Appearance Details

Additional visual details and characteristics.

**Example:**

```
Subtle reflections on the screen surface, slight lens flare on the 
camera module, pristine condition with no wear or scratches
```

**Tips:**

- Mention reflections and highlights
- Describe any special effects (lens flare, glow)
- Note condition and wear
- Include any branding or text visible

#### Removing Objects

Click the **"Remove Object"** button in the top-right corner of any object card to delete it.

## Complete Example

Here's a complete Pro Mode configuration for a luxury watch product shot:

### Scene & Style

**Short Description:**

```
A luxury Swiss automatic watch displayed on a reflective black surface
```

**Background Setting:**

```
High-end studio environment with a glossy black acrylic surface creating 
mirror-like reflections. Dark gradient background transitioning from 
charcoal to deep black. Minimal, sophisticated atmosphere.
```

**Style Medium:** `Luxury product photography`

**Artistic Style:** `Elegant and sophisticated with dramatic lighting`

**Context:**

```
Premium catalog hero shot emphasizing craftsmanship and luxury positioning 
for high-end watch collectors
```

### Lighting

**Conditions:** `Dramatic studio lighting with controlled highlights`

**Direction:** `45-degree side lighting from camera left with subtle fill from right`

**Shadows:** `Defined shadows with gradual falloff, creating depth and dimension`

### Aesthetics

**Composition:** `Centered composition with watch at slight angle, negative space above`

**Color Scheme:** `Monochromatic blacks and silvers with warm gold accents from watch`

**Mood & Atmosphere:** `Luxurious, exclusive, sophisticated, and timeless`

### Camera

**Camera Angle:** `Slight overhead angle at 20 degrees, three-quarter view`

**Lens & Focal Length:** `100mm macro lens for detail and compression`

**Depth of Field:** `Medium depth of field, f/5.6, watch face sharp, edges softly fading`

**Focus:** `Sharp focus on watch dial and hands, gradual falloff to case edges`

### Objects

**Object 1:**

- **Description:** `Swiss automatic watch with stainless steel case, blue sunburst dial, and brown leather strap`
- **Location:** `Center of frame on reflective surface`
- **Relationship:** `Main subject, hero product`
- **Relative Size:** `Large, filling approximately 60% of frame height`
- **Shape & Color:** `Circular case, 42mm diameter, polished steel with blue dial and brown leather`
- **Texture:** `Polished stainless steel case, sunburst brushed dial, textured leather strap`
- **Appearance Details:** `Visible watch hands at 10:10 position, applied indices catching light, subtle reflections on crystal, pristine condition`

## Tips for Effective Prompts

### Be Specific

Instead of "nice lighting," use "soft diffused studio lighting from 45-degree angle with minimal shadows"

### Use Professional Terminology

Reference real photography concepts: f-stops, focal lengths, lighting setups, composition rules

### Layer Your Details

Start with broad strokes (overall scene) and add specific details (exact colors, materials, finishes)

### Think Like a Photographer

Consider how a professional photographer would set up the shot: lighting, camera position, lens choice

### Maintain Consistency

Ensure all sections work together cohesively - lighting should match the mood, camera settings should support the composition

### Start Simple

Begin with one or two objects and basic settings, then add complexity as you become comfortable

### Iterate and Refine

Generate images, review results, and adjust specific parameters to fine-tune your output

## Keyboard Navigation

Pro Mode is fully keyboard accessible:

- **Tab**: Move between form fields
- **Shift + Tab**: Move backwards
- **Enter/Space**: Expand/collapse accordion sections
- **Enter**: Activate buttons (Generate, Add Object, Remove Object)

## Generating Your Image

1. Fill in all sections with your desired specifications
2. Add objects in the Object Builder (at least one recommended)
3. Click **"Generate Image"** at the bottom of the form
4. Wait for generation to complete (typically 30-60 seconds)
5. View your generated image in the results area

## Troubleshooting

### Generation Takes Too Long

- Complex scenes with multiple objects may take longer
- Wait up to 2 minutes before considering it a timeout
- Check your internet connection

### Unexpected Results

- Review each section for conflicting specifications
- Ensure lighting matches your desired mood
- Check that object descriptions are detailed enough
- Verify camera settings support your composition

### Error Messages

- **Validation Error**: Check that all required fields are filled
- **Network Error**: Check your internet connection
- **Server Error**: Try again in a few moments

## Best Practices

1. **Start with Scene & Style**: Establish the overall vision first
2. **Define Lighting Early**: Lighting dramatically affects the final result
3. **Be Consistent**: Ensure all sections support the same aesthetic vision
4. **Use Reference Language**: Think about real product photography you admire
5. **Iterate**: Generate, review, adjust, and regenerate for best results
6. **Save Successful Configs**: Keep notes on configurations that work well
7. **Experiment**: Try different combinations to discover what works best
